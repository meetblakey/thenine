# The Nine Product Strategy Brief

## Vision

The Nine is the group-only dating app that helps verified friend groups meet other verified groups in real life. The product exists to make dating feel less like solitary evaluation and more like a credible social plan.

## Problem

Dating apps still have demand, but the dominant architecture is failing users: endless individual feeds create decision overload, unclear intent, rejection loops, safety anxiety, and monetization distrust. The research shows low current usage relative to lifetime trial, rising burnout, and better growth for products that improve trust, intent density, and real-world conversion.

## Opportunity

The market is moving toward verified identity, friend-involved discovery, curated introductions, and offline experiences. Tinder Double Date validates quartet demand; Bumble Plans validates six-to-eight-person social pods; Hinge validates outcome-oriented conversation design; offline event growth validates appetite for lower-pressure real-world formats. No scaled product owns "verified group-first dating."

## Positioning

The Nine is for singles who want to meet romantically compatible people without doing solo swipe labor. It is positioned as verified, group-first, city-dense, and outcome-led. The app does not promise more matches. It promises better social conditions for saying yes.

## Target User

Initial users are 23-38 year-old urban singles in one dense launch city who already socialize in friend groups, are tired of solo dating apps, care about safety and authenticity, and are willing to meet through structured low-pressure plans.

## Core Product Bets

1. **The group is a better trust primitive than the solo profile.** Research basis: friend involvement is already normalized through Tinder Matchmaker behavior; Double Date, Doubble, PlotTwist, Tandem, and Bumble Plans show group formats lower pressure and create acquisition loops.
2. **Verification and intent clarity must happen before distribution.** Research basis: safety concerns are a major barrier, women are more likely to perceive app meetups as unsafe, and Tinder/Bumble are moving verification deeper into onboarding.
3. **Real-world group meetups beat engagement loops as the operating metric.** Research basis: fatigue is created by attention-heavy architecture, offline dating events are growing, and Hinge/Known evidence points toward optimizing for replies, dates, and attended outcomes.
